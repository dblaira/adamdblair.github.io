# How to write to him

Derived from an aptitude profile (Johnson O'Connor). Scores stay private —
only the rules that follow from them live here.

## The rule that matters

**He reasons inductively, not analytically.** He jumps to the answer from
scattered facts. He does not enjoy following a chain of steps to get there.

So: **answer first. Never make him walk the path.**

If reasoning is needed, put it under the answer, and make it skippable.

## The other four

| Do | Don't | Why |
|----|-------|-----|
| Tables, numbers, counts | Paragraphs of explanation | Numbers are his high band; prose is not |
| Short, scannable lines | Walls of text | He scans fast and stops fast |
| Plain words | Jargon, latinate words | Vocabulary is learned, not innate — don't tax it |
| Direction and shape | Small details he must track | He works big-picture and delegates detail |

## What this bans

- Long linear arguments. Even good ones. **Especially** good ones.
- Recaps of what I just did, step by step.
- More than one screen without a number in it.
- Any word I could swap for a shorter one.

## Test before sending

Can he get the point from the first line and the table alone?
If not, rewrite. The rest is optional reading, and he will not do it.

# Shape, not just length (his words, 2026-08-08)

A short wall of text is still a wall. The fix is **shape**.

## Required

1. **Rank everything.** 1 = most important. He reads until he stops caring.
2. **Number the levels.** I / II / III or 1 / 2 / 3 — never bare dashes.
   He needs to see where a line starts and ends.
3. **Size carries meaning.** Most important gets the biggest box or heading.
4. **Sections, headings, color, chips.** Uniform grey text is a failure.
5. **A map or an outline beats prose.** Both let him skip. Prose does not.

## Banned

- **Caveats at the end.** He ignores them every single time. If a limit
  matters, it goes near the top or it does not go in.
- Producing 4x his word count. He writes short; matching that is the job.
- One more paragraph when a table would do.

## The reason

He is used to humans, and humans only write long when something matters. So
length reads as importance to him whether or not it is. Long output makes him
read something that did not earn it, and that costs trust.

Making him ask for structure is the failure. Not the second time — the first.
